2027:
DOES THE WORLD LOOK THE SAME FROM SOMEWHERE ELSE?

The second edition deliberately widens the Almanac beyond its predominantly British and Western starting point. Every relevant chapter asks whether evidence from China and East Asia materially changes, qualifies or strengthens the Western interpretation.

International comparison is included only when it adds explanatory power. One deeply researched comparison is preferable to six superficial national statistics.

02

2027 is the first deliberate international expansion of the series. Asia is never treated as a homogeneous bloc.

The Eastern lens

The default East Asian comparison group is China, Japan and South Korea. Taiwan, Hong Kong and Singapore are included where statistically or culturally relevant. India and Southeast Asia may also be used where the subject requires them.

The aim is not to bolt an “Asian paragraph” onto every chapter. The research question is narrower and more demanding: does the subject under examination show materially different evidence, consequences, institutional responses or human outcomes elsewhere?

Where comparison matters

The recurring domains include artificial intelligence, employment, automation, money, housing, cost of living, education, fertility, family formation, relationships, loneliness, health, ageing, religion, meaning, social cohesion, information environments and governance.

Evidence standard

Primary national statistical and regulatory sources are used wherever possible and cross-checked against international organisations, peer-reviewed research and credible independent analysis. Chinese official statistics are neither dismissed automatically nor treated as the sole interpretation.

The same triangulation standard applies to every country: what does the official record say, what do independent sources say, where do the measures disagree, and what do the differences mean for actual human outcomes?

Non-dilution principle

More geography must not mean less research.

Broader geographical coverage is useful only when it sharpens explanation. The 2027 edition favours fewer, stronger comparisons over decorative internationalism.

Compare systems

Look beyond headline statistics to institutions, incentives, regulation, demographics and social structure.

Compare consequences

Ask whether the same technological development produces different effects on work, family life, wellbeing or social trust.

Keep the evidence visible

Separate measured fact, credible inference, disagreement and forecast rather than blending them into one narrative.
