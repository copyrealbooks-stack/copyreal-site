2027:
DOES THE WORLD LOOK THE SAME FROM SOMEWHERE ELSE?

The second edition makes the reader's map more useful by testing it beyond a mainly British and Western starting point. Every relevant chapter asks whether evidence from China and East Asia changes, qualifies or strengthens the picture — and whether that difference matters to the choices people are making in everyday life.

The purpose of international comparison is not to collect countries. It is to help the reader see which changes are local, which are global and which systems produce genuinely different outcomes.

02

2027 is the first deliberate international expansion of the series. Asia is never treated as a homogeneous bloc.

A wider map, not a different mission

The Almanac remains a practical guide to navigating rapid change. The 2027 edition simply makes that guide harder to distort by one country's assumptions. If work, housing, family life, education, technology or social trust are changing differently elsewhere, the reader should know why.

The Eastern lens

The default East Asian comparison group is China, Japan and South Korea. Taiwan, Hong Kong and Singapore are included where statistically or culturally relevant. India and Southeast Asia may also be used where the subject requires them.

The aim is not to bolt an “Asian paragraph” onto every chapter. The research question is narrower and more demanding: does the subject under examination show materially different evidence, consequences, institutional responses or human outcomes elsewhere?

Where comparison matters

The recurring domains include employment, automation, money, housing, cost of living, education, fertility, family formation, relationships, loneliness, health, ageing, religion, meaning, social cohesion, information environments, governance and artificial intelligence where it materially affects those areas.

Evidence standard

Primary national statistical and regulatory sources are used wherever possible and cross-checked against international organisations, peer-reviewed research and credible independent analysis. Chinese official statistics are neither dismissed automatically nor treated as the sole interpretation.

The same triangulation standard applies to every country: what does the official record say, what do independent sources say, where do the measures disagree, and what do the differences mean for actual human outcomes?

Non-dilution principle

More geography must not mean less usefulness.

Broader geographical coverage earns its place only when it helps the reader understand the world more clearly. One strong comparison that changes the conclusion is worth more than six decorative statistics.

Compare systems

Look beyond headlines to institutions, incentives, regulation, demographics and social structure.

Compare consequences

Ask whether the same change produces different effects on work, family life, wellbeing, opportunity or trust.

Return to the reader

Every comparison must answer a practical question: what does knowing this help a person understand, anticipate or decide?
