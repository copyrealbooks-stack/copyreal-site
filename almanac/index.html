This is not another book about A.I. It is about your life in a world being reshaped by technology, economics, politics, culture and social change: what is happening, what it could mean for you, and how to make better decisions without drowning in noise.

What is it for?
YOU

You do not need to become an A.I. expert. You need a clear view of which changes may affect your job, income, rent, education, relationships, health, attention and plans — and which headlines can safely be ignored.

The point

A map for real life.

The Almanac is built for the person trying to work out how to live, choose and plan while the ground keeps moving. It translates large, messy changes into questions that matter in ordinary life.

What is actually changing?

Separate durable change from hype, panic, sales pitches and the story of the week.

What does it mean for me?

Connect big trends to work, money, housing, education, relationships, health, freedom and opportunity.

What should I do with that?

Give the reader enough context to make better choices without pretending anyone can predict the future perfectly.

The A.I. in the title is part of the method. A.I. is used to help interrogate evidence, compare systems, surface contradictions and keep the map current. The destination is human understanding, not technology worship.

Annual editions

One series.
A changing map.

Each edition preserves the previous year's baseline and asks a harder question. The aim is to help readers orient themselves as the world changes, rather than discovering years later that the rules changed while nobody was looking.

Your world, not just A.I.

A.I. as a navigation instrument

Use the technology to understand the age.

The Almanac deliberately uses A.I. in the research process to help examine large bodies of evidence, compare countries and systems, identify contradictions, test assumptions and follow fast-moving developments. Human editorial judgement decides what belongs, what remains uncertain and what matters to the reader.

Find the signal

Use A.I. to search across complex evidence and expose connections that are easy to miss when subjects are studied in isolation.

Challenge the answer

Claims are checked against primary sources, independent evidence and competing interpretations rather than accepted because a model produced them.

Bring it back to life

The final question is always practical: what does this change for a person trying to build a workable life?
