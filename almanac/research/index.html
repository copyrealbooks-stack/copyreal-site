Method · Sources · Interpretation

RESEARCH
STANDARD

The research exists to make the world easier to navigate. The Almanac uses A.I. as an active research instrument to help process, compare and challenge large bodies of evidence, while keeping source checking, uncertainty and editorial judgement visible.

Why use A.I. this way?

Because the problem is too much information, not too little.

Important changes now unfold across technology, economics, politics, health, culture, education and social life at the same time. A.I. can help connect those fields, search for contradictions and keep a large evidence map current. It is a tool for orientation, not an authority.

Search wider

Use A.I. to interrogate large collections of sources and identify connections that a single-subject reading may miss.

Compare faster

Test how the same issue looks across countries, institutions, datasets and time periods without assuming one system is universal.

Challenge claims

Use contradictions as prompts for deeper checking rather than smoothing them away into a convenient answer.

Return to the reader

Ask what the evidence changes for work, money, housing, relationships, health, trust, opportunity and everyday decisions.

Source hierarchy

Start with evidence closest to the event.

A.I.-assisted discovery is never treated as evidence by itself. Where possible, claims begin with primary statistical, regulatory, institutional or technical material and are then cross-checked against independent and international sources.

PrimaryNational statistics, regulators, legislation, public datasets, company filings, technical documentationEstablish what was measured, announced or formally changed.

Editorial tests

Four questions before a claim survives.

Not every interesting statistic belongs in the Almanac. Evidence is retained when it survives tests of relevance, comparability, corroboration and material significance.

1. What exactly was measured?

Definitions, sample, geography, period and methodology matter. Similar labels can conceal different measures.

2. Does another source agree?

Agreement increases confidence. Disagreement is not discarded; it becomes part of the analysis.

3. Does comparison add anything?

A second country is included only when it changes, qualifies or strengthens the interpretation.

4. What changes for people?

The final test is material consequence: work, income, housing, family life, health, trust, opportunity, autonomy or meaning.

Evidence labelling

Fact is not forecast.

Fast-moving subjects become misleading when observation, inference and prediction are written in the same voice. The Almanac keeps those categories distinct so the reader can tell what is known, what is inferred and what remains a bet on the future.

Observed

Directly supported by current evidence or a documented event.

Inferred

A reasoned interpretation supported by evidence but not directly measured as stated.

Forecast

A forward-looking judgement that may be useful, but remains contingent and revisable.

∞

The standard stays the same as the map grows: use more information to create more clarity, not more noise.

International comparison

The 2027 edition introduces a recurring China and East Asia lens. It does not assume that Western evidence generalises globally, nor that differences elsewhere automatically invalidate the Western case.

Asia is not treated as a single unit. China, Japan and South Korea form the default East Asian comparison group; Taiwan, Hong Kong and Singapore are added when statistically or culturally relevant, with India and Southeast Asia used where the subject requires them.

Non-dilution

Broader geographical coverage must not produce shallower research.

The Almanac therefore favours deep comparisons over national-statistic collections. A country appears because its evidence helps explain the subject and improve the reader's map, not because a quota of territories needs to be represented.

Corrections and revision

An annual almanac is a snapshot, not a claim to permanent certainty. Later evidence may revise earlier conclusions. The series is designed so that those changes can be shown rather than hidden.
