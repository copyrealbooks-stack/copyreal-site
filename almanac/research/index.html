Method · Sources · Interpretation

RESEARCH
STANDARD

The Almanac is designed as a cumulative evidence record. It distinguishes observed change from interpretation and forecast, and it treats geographical comparison as a research tool rather than decoration.

Source hierarchy

Start with evidence closest to the event.

Where possible, claims begin with primary statistical, regulatory, institutional or technical evidence. Those sources are then cross-checked against independent and international material before an interpretation is treated as robust.

LayerTypical sourcesPurpose
PrimaryNational statistics, regulators, legislation, public datasets, company filings, technical documentationEstablish what was measured, announced or formally changed.
InternationalOECD, ILO, WHO, World Bank and comparable multilateral bodiesImprove comparability and provide external benchmarks.
AcademicPeer-reviewed research, major research institutes and longitudinal studiesTest mechanisms, causal claims and longer-run effects.
Independent analysisCredible specialist reporting, sector analysis and transparent datasetsChallenge official interpretation and add context unavailable in primary sources.
Editorial tests

Four questions before a claim survives.

Not every interesting statistic belongs in the Almanac. Evidence is retained when it survives tests of relevance, comparability, corroboration and material significance.

1. What exactly was measured?

Definitions, sample, geography, period and methodology matter. Similar labels can conceal different measures.

2. Does another source agree?

Agreement increases confidence. Disagreement is not discarded; it becomes part of the analysis.

3. Does comparison add anything?

A second country is included only when it changes, qualifies or strengthens the interpretation.

4. What changes for people?

The final test is material consequence: work, income, housing, family life, health, trust, opportunity, autonomy or meaning.

Evidence labelling

Fact is not forecast.

Fast-moving subjects become misleading when observation, inference and prediction are written in the same voice. The Almanac keeps those categories distinct.

Observed

Directly supported by current evidence or a documented event.

Inferred

A reasoned interpretation supported by evidence but not directly measured as stated.

Forecast

A forward-looking judgement that may be useful, but remains contingent and revisable.

∞

The research standard is intended to persist across annual editions even as the geographical scope expands.

International comparison

The 2027 edition introduces a recurring China and East Asia lens. It does not assume that Western evidence generalises globally, nor that differences elsewhere automatically invalidate the Western case.

Asia is not treated as a single unit. China, Japan and South Korea form the default East Asian comparison group; Taiwan, Hong Kong and Singapore are added when statistically or culturally relevant, with India and Southeast Asia used where the subject requires them.

Non-dilution

Broader geographical coverage must not produce shallower research.

The Almanac therefore favours deep comparisons over national-statistic collections. A country appears because its evidence helps explain the subject, not because a quota of territories needs to be represented.

Corrections and revision

An annual almanac is a snapshot, not a claim to permanent certainty. Later evidence may revise earlier conclusions. The series is designed so that those changes can be shown rather than hidden.
